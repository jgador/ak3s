# Language and terminology

Use plain international English throughout the repository, including documentation, comments, user-facing text, issues, pull requests, and generated content.

- Prefer simple, literal, widely understood words.
- Avoid jargon when an ordinary word communicates the same meaning accurately.
- Use technical terminology when it is necessary for precision or is widely established in the relevant industry or technology.
- Do not replace established technical terms, protocol names, API concepts, library terminology, or standard computing terms merely to make them simpler.
- Avoid US-specific, UK-specific, or other regional expressions, idioms, slang, cultural references, and figures of speech.
- Avoid business and marketing jargon unless it is an established term required by the domain.
- Prefer internationally understood wording over region-specific alternatives.
- Prefer direct verbs and concrete nouns over abstract or fashionable wording.
- Keep sentences concise, but do not sacrifice technical accuracy.
- When a specialized term is necessary and may not be familiar to the intended reader, explain it briefly on first use.
- Preserve terminology defined by external specifications, APIs, standards, libraries, tools, and upstream projects.

Examples:

- Prefer `use` over `leverage` or `utilize`.
- Prefer `start` over `kick off`.
- Prefer `investigate` over `dig into`.
- Prefer `temporary solution` over `band-aid`.
- Prefer `main requirement` over `north star`.
- Prefer `works automatically` over `works like magic`.
- Prefer `internal implementation` over `under the hood`.
- Keep technical terms such as `HTTP`, `JSON`, `dependency injection`, `race condition`, `idempotent`, `Kubernetes`, and `garbage collection` when they are technically correct.

When choosing between simpler wording and an established technical term, prioritize precision. Do not invent simpler terminology that could make the technical meaning ambiguous.
